# Supermarket Product Placement Optimisation

> Probabilistic modelling and revenue optimisation of supermarket product placement.

## Overview

This project investigates how the spatial arrangement of products in a supermarket can influence purchasing behaviour and expected revenue.

The project was developed as part of a French scientific research project (TIPE).

The approach combines probabilistic modelling, simulation and optimisation to evaluate different product placement strategies.

## Objective

The objective is to determine a product layout that maximises the expected revenue generated by a typical shopping basket.

The project consists of:

- constructing a representative shopping basket from publicly available information;
- modelling purchasing relationships between products;
- assigning probabilities to different purchasing behaviours;
- simulating different product placement strategies;
- comparing layouts according to their expected revenue.

## Data and assumptions

No real customer transaction data were available for this project.

Instead, publicly available information published by supermarkets was used to construct a representative shopping basket and define realistic purchasing patterns.

The remaining data used by the model were constructed specifically for the simulation, based on these assumptions.

This approach made it possible to study the optimisation problem while avoiding the use of confidential or personally identifiable customer data.

## Methodology

### 1. Construction of a typical shopping basket

A representative shopping basket was constructed using publicly available information from supermarkets.

The basket contains a selection of products intended to represent a realistic shopping scenario.

### 2. Modelling purchasing relationships

Relationships between products were modelled to represent the likelihood of purchasing one product depending on the presence of others.

### 3. Probabilistic model

These relationships were converted into probabilities describing different possible purchasing behaviours.

### 4. Placement simulation

Different spatial arrangements of products were simulated.

For each placement, the model evaluates the resulting purchasing behaviour and calculates the corresponding expected revenue.

### 5. Optimisation

The optimal placement is defined as the layout that maximises the expected revenue of the supermarket.

## Results

The model makes it possible to quantitatively compare different product placement strategies.

The main outputs include:

- a probabilistic representation of purchasing behaviour;
- simulated product placement strategies;
- expected revenue associated with each strategy;
- identification of the placement strategy maximising expected revenue.
